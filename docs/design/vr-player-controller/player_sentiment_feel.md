# Player and critic sentiment on VR player-controller "feel" (acclaimed VR action games + flat-to-VR mods), plus haptic and audio feedback practice

> A research snapshot (2026-10-21), not a description of the framework's code: what was built is in
> `docs/`.

Scope note: researched 2026-10-21. About 45 search/fetch calls. Sources range from 2016 to October 2026. Where a claim comes only from a search-engine excerpt and not a full-page fetch, it is marked "(excerpt)". Machine-generated review aggregators (vaporlens, gamegeeker, nodal) are flagged as weak signals. "Consensus" means several independent outlets or many users agree. "Individual" means one reviewer or one poster.

## Case studies: what players and reviewers praised and criticised (hands, body, guns, reloading, movement, comfort)

### Takeaway
Across the acclaimed titles, the same things earn praise: hands that follow the player 1:1 with believable finger poses, manual multi-step reloads that feel tactile, a free hand always available, and interactions that need no button lookup. The same things get criticised: physics bodies that snag on geometry, grabs that need several attempts, inventory and holster slots that grab the wrong item or are hard to reach, reload complexity under pressure, and sickness from fast artificial motion. The Titanfall 2 VR mod (CircuitLord, Oct 2026) is praised as "native-feeling" largely because it fixed the classic flat-to-VR failures: articulated hands, per-gun manual reloads, physical punching, VR-specific button prompts, and a 2D option for cutscenes.

### Cited Findings

#### Titanfall 2 VR mod (CircuitLord, released ~early Oct 2026)
- Road to VR (Scott Hayden, 2 Oct 2026): a full conversion with head tracking, weapon aiming, mech weapon controls and manual reloading across the full weapon roster. It has a gesture-based reload for guns like the Wingman, which the article ties to Ben Lang's earlier Wingman VR experiment. It covers single-player only. — [Road to VR](https://roadtovr.com/titanfall-2-vr-mod-circuitlord/)
- Road to VR on comfort: "The amount of motion in the game goes above and beyond even a regular flatscreen shooter." Snap turn is included, but "there's only so much that can be done to mitigate the potential motion sickness." The reviewer says it is "not going to be for everyone—or probably even most people." — [Road to VR](https://roadtovr.com/titanfall-2-vr-mod-circuitlord/)
- Aftermath (Chris Person, 7 Oct 2026) on hands and body: fingers and limbs are fully articulated, "Your character's hands follow your own hands," and BT's hands mirror the pilot's. Melee means making a fist and physically punching, and sucker punches from cloak are "always a blast." — [Aftermath](https://aftermath.site/titanfall-2-vr-mod-circuitlord/)
- Aftermath on reloading: eject the mag, take a fresh one from the backpack, insert it, then cock with the off hand. The method varies by gun: Mastiff shells load one at a time, and the Kraber needs a reach over the gun to work the bolt. "Reloading by hand takes some getting used to." "The Kraber is a huge pain in the ass to use. It should be." — [Aftermath](https://aftermath.site/titanfall-2-vr-mod-circuitlord/)
- Aftermath on weapons and aiming: two-handing is "intrinsically tricky," but the secondary grip can hold the gun to free the primary hand. Holographic sights "function correctly" with "shocking" depth, while real scopes have a shadow that makes aiming harder. You can blind-fire shotguns around corners. Some abilities (Ion's laser) are aimed with the head. — [Aftermath](https://aftermath.site/titanfall-2-vr-mod-circuitlord/)
- Aftermath criticisms: weapon juggling is "a little tricky." The reviewer wants hip-switching or Alyx-style switching. Secondary abilities use grip plus a hand movement in one of four directions, which "takes a bit of getting used to." — [Aftermath](https://aftermath.site/titanfall-2-vr-mod-circuitlord/)
- Aftermath on movement and comfort: wall running "feels surprisingly organic." Crouch and slide work by stick flick or by physically crouching. The reviewer "never once got motion sick," but found forced-VR boss-intro cutscene cameras intense enough to agree with the developer's warning. On the Titan: "lumbering and powerful in the correct ways," and "I felt genuinely present inside BT's hull." — [Aftermath](https://aftermath.site/titanfall-2-vr-mod-circuitlord/)
- UploadVR (Mike Johnson, 6 Oct 2026): in-game prompts were remapped to VR controllers instead of showing LB/RB/L3/R3, which is part of why it "feels like a native VR game." A toggle plays cutscenes either in full VR (with "camera jerkiness") or on a 2D panel for sickness-prone players. The one-click installer is the headline praise. Johnson calls it "a finished product out of the gate." — [UploadVR](https://www.uploadvr.com/titanfall-2s-vr-mod-is-stellar-and-its-installer-is-the-best-thing-about-it/)
- 6DOF Reviews is positive: its reviewer spent about 8 hours in the campaign without feeling the game didn't belong in a headset (excerpt; the page returned HTTP 429). — [6DOF Reviews](https://www.6dofreviews.com/reviews/titanfall-2-vr)
- ResetEra (individuals): one hands-on poster said "Interaction design is very very good for a mod" and "If you close your fist and physically punch, it works in VR!" They rated sickness for a susceptible player at "maybe 8 out of 10," with Titan sections gentler than on-foot play. Another poster says it needs a laser sight because there is no crosshair and ADS at speed is hard. About 5 posters voiced sickness worries. Others said it "feels like native VR support rather than a mod" and is "much more polished" than other VR mods. — [ResetEra](https://www.resetera.com/threads/titanfall-2-vr-mod-is-now-available.1644223/)
- Conflict: Road to VR expects most people can't handle the motion, while Aftermath's reviewer never got sick. Sickness tolerance is individual, but every outlet flags it.

#### Half-Life: Alyx
- Valve's Kerry Davis said the gravity gloves were made always-on rather than a separate weapon so players use them naturally. Buttons and switches were treated as the "least VR thing you can do." Distance grab matters because "movement is sort of one of the highest friction areas" of VR. The goal was gloves that are "intuitive, automatic... and something that won't wear out its welcome." (excerpt) — [DigiPen](https://www.digipen.edu/showcase/news/with-half-life-alyx-digipen-graduate-kerry-davis-helps-redefine-vr-games); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2020-03-31-half-life-alyx-valve-interview-vr.html)
- Valve AMA (excerpt): Valve wanted simultaneous two-handed play throughout, so the player always needs an easily free hand. No arms are rendered because players "don't notice them missing" (spectators do), and arms would obscure the view. — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/half-life-alyx-ama-everything-we-learned)
- Ben Lang (Road to VR, republished 23 Mar 2026) on force-pull: it has three stages (select with a glow, lock with the trigger, confirm with a flick gesture), and each stage has its own sound. The trajectory adapts in real time to reach the hand rather than snapping. Throwing ignores inertia sway "only in its throwing calculation," so heavy objects still throw accurately. — [Road to VR](https://www.roadtovr.com/these-details-make-half-life-alyx-unlike-any-other-vr-game-inside-xr-design/)
- Road to VR (Lang) on readable state: pistol mags show the remaining count with a light per bullet plus a radial indicator, and "Completely empty magazines emit no light." Menus accept both laser pointing and touch. Key items get subtle lights instead of yellow outlines, which is "much less immersion breaking." — [Road to VR](https://www.roadtovr.com/these-details-make-half-life-alyx-unlike-any-other-vr-game-inside-xr-design/)
- Praise for reloading tension: ejecting, grabbing ammo from the backpack, loading and racking is "nerve-wracking" in a good way. — [Universal-Sci](https://www.universal-sci.com/sci-gaming/half-life-alyx-is-still-a-masterpiece)
- Criticisms (professional): you only get three weapons, and the pistol and pulse rifle overlap in role as they are upgraded. — [Road to VR review](https://www.roadtovr.com/half-life-alyx-review/). UploadVR argued Valve held back physicality, especially melee and fully interactive arm's-length environments, compared with Boneworks and Saints & Sinners. — [UploadVR](https://uploadvr.com/half-life-alyx-editorial-one-year/). PCWorld's cons: "doesn't do much we haven't seen" and "slow to start." — [PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/398913)
- Criticism (individual): one user called reloading awkward because each of the three guns has a completely different reload action. — [Backloggd](https://backloggd.com/u/LucaZ93/reviews/)

#### Boneworks / Bonelab (Stress Level Zero)
- Boneworks praise (Road to VR / Cas & Chary, Dec 2019): items are "grabbable on far more places," which allows underhand or overhand dagger grips. Force-grab saves crouching. A one-handed sledgehammer is "almost impossible" to aim accurately, so weight changes handling. Inventory "shows you the open slots on your body." You can improvise by throwing a gun and switching to fists. — [Road to VR](https://roadtovr.com/boneworks-physics-driven-mechanics-gameplay-overview/)
- Boneworks criticism (same source): "I could get my arms or body stuck between two pipes, and I would struggle to get myself out." Awkward physics moments make it "pretty intense comfort-wise," and it is hard to play long without sickness. — [Road to VR](https://roadtovr.com/boneworks-physics-driven-mechanics-gameplay-overview/)
- Launch-week r/ValveIndex (individuals): "IK is janky but not as janky as it could have been." Another called it "a ridiculous amount of jank and glitch." A third complained that the save system blocked the breaks that sickness-inducing design required (excerpt). — [r/ValveIndex megathread mirror](https://lr.ggtyler.dev/r/ValveIndex/comments/e8pq0j/boneworks_megathread/fafxrev)
- Aggregate verdict (weak, aggregator): "the physics nails it; the price is a thin story, shallow combat, and real motion sickness." Steam is about 92% positive across more than 34k reviews. — [GameGeeker](https://gamegeeker.com/games/boneworks/review?market=US)
- Bonelab criticisms: hand physics are "sometimes oddly wonky," and gripping and climbing can take several attempts. — [Plugged In](https://www.pluggedin.com/game-reviews/bonelab/). Control inaccuracies caused "annoying falls into the depths while climbing," and obstacles cause "a silly twitching of the hand." Body-swap is a highlight but unlocks late. — [Mixed](https://mixed-news.com/en/bonelab-review-vr-shooter-hit-or-failed-experiment/). - Bonelab praise: full-body physics are "an incredible step forward for VR." — [Gaming Trend](https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/bonelab-review-the-future-isnt-here-yet/). PC Gamer calls it playful and creative but intense for newcomers. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/bonelab-review/)

### Inferences
- The strongest positive signal across every case is hand fidelity (1:1 tracking, believable finger wrap, a free off-hand) plus diegetic, readable state (Alyx mag lights, HL2VR on-gun HUD, Idle Hands back-of-hand HUD). A generic runtime should treat hand pose quality and a free-hand guarantee as tier-0 features.
- Manual reloads are a net positive in reviews when (a) the motions are learnable within about an hour (Pavlov, AS2), (b) ammo sources are reliable to grab, and (c) there is an escape hatch (HL2VR quick reload, Hard Bullet arcade mode, AS2 relaxed steps). The complaints cluster where ammo access misfires (AS2 pouch, ITR belt) or volume of enemies outstrips reload speed (Metro).
- Titanfall 2 VR shows that a mod can reach "native" reception if it removes flatscreen tells: gamepad glyphs, forced camera cuts and head-aimed weapons.

- I found no critical professional review of Hard Bullet, and only aggregator-level data on H3VR.

## Recurring "jank" complaints: which are worst?

### Takeaway
The most damaging complaints are (1) sickness from artificial or fast motion, which is the deal-breaker that excludes people outright, and (2) failed or wrong grabs during time-pressured actions (reloads, holsters, climbing), which repeatedly frustrate even tolerant players. Physics-body snagging and hand twitching are the signature failures of physics-hand games. Gun-to-hand misalignment and long-range jitter are smaller but persistent. Floaty weight and throwing are contested design trade-offs rather than pure bugs. A University of Alberta study of Steam reviews found that game-specific complaints now outnumber cybersickness complaints, so interaction quality is increasingly where games are judged.

### Cited Findings
- University of Alberta analysis of 750 popular VR games and 17,635 Steam reviews: game-specific issues have become the most frequent complaint type, while cybersickness complaints are fading (excerpt). — [UAlberta ASGAARD lab](https://asgaard.ece.ualberta.ca/an-empirical-study-of-trends-of-popular-virtual-reality-games-and-their-complaints-was-accepted-for-publication-in-the-tg-journal/)
- **Sickness and comfort (consensus that it's the biggest barrier for high-motion games):** Titanfall 2 VR is "not going to be for everyone—or probably even most people." — [Road to VR](https://roadtovr.com/titanfall-2-vr-mod-circuitlord/). Bonelab "will almost certainly make you sick within minutes." — [Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/gaming/virtual-reality/bonelab-review-the-new-standard-for-vr-physics-based-games). Boneworks is hard to play long. — [Road to VR](https://roadtovr.com/boneworks-physics-driven-mechanics-gameplay-overview/). Forced cutscene cameras are a sickness trigger, and a 2D-panel fallback is offered. — [UploadVR](https://www.uploadvr.com/titanfall-2s-vr-mod-is-stellar-and-its-installer-is-the-best-thing-about-it/); [Aftermath](https://aftermath.site/titanfall-2-vr-mod-circuitlord/). Lone Echo II was criticised for smooth-only locomotion with limited comfort options. — [Road to VR](https://roadtovr.com/lone-echo-2-review-oculus-rift-quest/)
- **Physics body or hands snagging on geometry:** arms or body get "stuck between two pipes." — [Road to VR](https://roadtovr.com/boneworks-physics-driven-mechanics-gameplay-overview/). Obstacles cause "a silly twitching of the hand," and climbing inaccuracy causes falls. — [Mixed](https://mixed-news.com/en/bonelab-review-vr-shooter-hit-or-failed-experiment/). ### Inferences
- Rough severity ranking for a runtime engineer, from the evidence: (1) sickness triggers from camera motion the player didn't cause (forced cutscene cameras, high-speed locomotion, physics-body jolts); (2) unreliable grabs and ammo or holster misfires under time pressure; (3) the hand or weapon pose not matching the real controller (angle offsets per controller model); (4) physics bodies snagging on or twitching against geometry; (5) jitter at range; (6) weight and throw tuning. Items 1 to 3 are runtime-level and game-agnostic, so they are worth solving first. Items 4 to 6 are partly per-game tuning.
- Grip pose offsets differ per controller model (Index, Quest 3, Quest Pro). A runtime needs per-controller grip-to-weapon calibration and a user-adjustable offset, because players already reach for external tools such as OpenVR Input Emulator.
- Ghost hands with a visible collision proxy are the safest default for a generic runtime. Physics hands only pay off where grab poses are authored, which a game-agnostic injector usually can't guarantee.

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative ranking of jank complaint types beyond the UAlberta headline. The study's per-category percentages were not retrieved.
- I found no direct sources on "hands clipping through walls" complaints in specific named games (only the ghost/physics debate).

## Haptics: which events get haptics, pulse parameters, Valve/Meta guidance

### Takeaway
Platform guidance converges on short, sharp transients tied 1:1 to a causal action (hover, grab, release, press, fire, impact), kept subtle, synchronised with audio, scaled with audio intensity, never long and overlapping in fast action, and always user-adjustable or mutable. Few concrete numbers are published. Meta's system pack gives the only explicit timings (Success: 2 events over 175 ms rising; Warning: 2 events over 150 ms falling). API ranges are amplitude 0–1, duration in seconds or nanoseconds, and frequency in Hz but often ignored or clamped. Legacy OpenVR limits re-triggering to once per 5 ms per controller axis.

### Cited Findings
- Meta principles: haptics should reinforce "a clear causal relationship between the haptic and the action." "Avoid diving into the production of haptics without a well-defined purpose." "Some of the most effective haptics are the ones that you only notice when they are missing." Pair haptics with audio and visuals so users perceive "one event." — [Meta Unity haptics design guidelines](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/documentation/unity/unity-haptics-design-guidelines/)
- Meta do's: make haptics adjustable and mutable, with the experience still working without them. Keep patterns consistent so they're learnable. Vary effects with input and AV context: "Softer sounds should correlate to softer haptics." Don'ts: overuse, and "long, overlapping effects, especially in fast-paced action games." Leave pauses, since haptics are "more impactful after a short pause." Haptics can produce audible noise when the controller touches a surface. — [Meta Unity haptics design guidelines](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/documentation/unity/unity-haptics-design-guidelines/)
- Meta device capabilities: Quest 2 has a narrowband LRA (single frequency, amplitude only). Quest Pro has a wideband VCM (frequency plus amplitude). Quest 3/3S have a wideband VCM with slightly lower output than Pro. — [Meta Unity haptics design guidelines](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/documentation/unity/unity-haptics-design-guidelines/)
- Meta Essential Pack (9 system effects): Hover is a short sharp transient at the edge of an interactive surface. Press mimics a non-latching button bounce. Long press marks the hold threshold. Select is two transients and Deselect is the reverse. Object hover is short and sharp, explicitly not continuous, "so it doesn't interfere with the grab." Grab conveys weight and resistance at contact. Release "mirrors Grab at lower amplitude and frequency." Success is 2 events over 175 ms ramping up. Warning is 2 events over 150 ms ramping down. Error is 4 abrupt events. — [Meta haptics system](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/design/haptics-system/)
- OpenXR `XrHapticVibration`: duration is in nanoseconds, and `XR_MIN_HAPTIC_DURATION` requests the shortest pulse the device supports. Amplitude is 0.0–1.0. Frequency is in Hz. Duration and frequency may be clamped to implementation-dependent ranges. — [Khronos OpenXR spec](https://registry.khronos.org/OpenXR/specs/1.1/man/html/XrHapticVibration.html). On Quest the simple API ignores `frequency`, so frequency control needs the parametric or PCM haptics extensions. — [Meta OpenXR haptics](https://developers.meta.com/vr/documentation/native/android/mobile-openxr-haptic/)
- SteamVR Input: `TriggerHapticVibrationAction(action, fStartSecondsFromNow, fDurationSeconds, fFrequency, fAmplitude)`, where amplitude "must be between 0.0 and 1.0." No recommended values are given. — [OpenVR wiki SteamVR Input](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/openvr/wiki/SteamVR-Input). Legacy `IVRSystem::TriggerHapticPulse` takes a duration in microseconds (unsigned short), and the app "may not trigger another haptic pulse on this controller and axis combination for 5ms." — [OpenVR wiki](https://github.com/ValveSoftware/openvr/wiki/IVRSystem::TriggerHapticPulse)
- Unity XRI Simple Haptic Feedback exposes events for Select Entered/Exited/Canceled and Hover Entered/Exited/Canceled, each with amplitude, duration and frequency. A frequency of 0 means the device default. Default amplitude and duration were not documented on the page. — [Unity XRI docs](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.xr.interaction.toolkit@3.0/manual/simple-haptic-feedback.html)
- VR Utility Belt (generic OpenVR wrapper) defaults: strength 200, interval 5 ms, duration 100 ms. These show the pulse-train structure and are not tuned values. — [VR Utility Belt docs](https://vrutilitybelt.readthedocs.io/en/latest/js/api/haptics.html)
- Haptek (ex-Striker VR) builds per-weapon effects from discrete events: "single shot, out-of-ammo, three round burst, chain saw, pulse rifle." — [Road to VR](https://roadtovr.com/striker-vr-arena-infinity-haptic-vr-gun-force-feedback-effects/). A patent notes firing-mechanism motion of about 15 ms is too fast for conventional VR tracking to detect, which shows how short a shot event is. — [US12510319B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12510319)
- Flat-to-VR: advanced UEVR profiles in 2026 add recoil and haptics, alongside hands, holsters, gestures and manual reload. — [PCVR Central](https://pcvrcentral.com/guides/best-uevr-games-2026)

### Inferences
- A sensible default event map for a generic runtime, consistent with Meta's pack and Unity XRI's event set: hover-over-grabbable (tiny transient), grab (stronger transient, scaled by object mass if known), release (weaker mirror of grab), holster in and out (transient), mag insert, mag eject and slide rack (distinct transients per step, because reload steps are where grab failures happen and confirmation matters most), fire (sharp attack, fast decay, scaled per weapon class), empty click (distinct pattern), damage taken (longer and distinct), and surface contact or collision (only if physics hands exist).
- Because Quest 2 ignores frequency and OpenXR may clamp, the runtime should express effects as amplitude envelopes first, with frequency as optional enhancement. It also needs a rate limiter (at least 5 ms between pulses per hand) and a global intensity slider with mute, per Meta's guidance.
- Use the shortest-possible pulse (`XR_MIN_HAPTIC_DURATION`) for "tick" confirmations. Reserve longer patterns for rare events so fast fights don't produce overlapping buzz.

### Gaps
- I found no published per-event haptic tables (durations or amplitudes) from Valve (Alyx), Stress Level Zero or nDreams. Valve gives API shape only, and no Valve design guidance on haptics was found.
- I found no reviewer commentary on haptics in Titanfall 2 VR, Alyx, Boneworks or the other case studies. Reviewers rarely mention haptics unless absent, which is consistent with Meta's "only notice when missing" line, but that is inference.
- Meta's separate "Haptics best practices" page was not fetched. Unity XRI default amplitude and duration values were not found.

## Audio feedback for interactions (grab, holster, mag insert, rack) and why it matters

### Takeaway
Valve's Alyx team treated interaction audio as core to presence. Grab sounds are deliberately few and consistent for clarity. Nearly every prop makes velocity-driven, spatialised sound when moved, shaken or dropped. The gravity gloves have a layered sound per interaction stage, and player-body Foley sells physical presence because players see only their hands. Close-up sounds needed rework because VR invites inspecting objects at ear distance. Audio and haptics should fire as one synchronised event.

### Cited Findings
- Roland Shaw (Valve): "There are only a few hand grab sounds — a deliberate decision to provide clear familiar feedback." "Picking up most props and moving them will result in relevant audio feedback" (aerosol cans, matches, ammo in clips). Impacts have "many thousands of possible variations" through "velocity driven layering." Testers asked for a louder landing sound, which Shaw took as a sign of physical presence. — [A Sound Effect](https://asoundeffect.com/half-life-alyx-vr-sound/)
- Dave Feise (Valve) on gravity gloves: a hand loop, a rollover sound from the targeted object, loops travelling from hand to object, a ping on hand close, and a second ping on the wrist flick. — [A Sound Effect](https://asoundeffect.com/half-life-alyx-vr-sound/)
- Feise on weapons: each shot is four layers (Head, Body, LFE, Tail) with 3–5 random variations per layer. Head and Body go through HRTF for position, while LFE and Tail skip it for spread. Simple old sounds localise poorly under HRTF and were reworked to add frequency content. Close-up inspection in VR is "so much more pronounced than in a flat-screen game," so close sounds were rethought. — [A Sound Effect](https://asoundeffect.com/half-life-alyx-vr-sound/)
- Shaw: testers stuck their heads into animated objects "expecting to hear suitable feedback and intricate details." — [A Sound Effect](https://asoundeffect.com/half-life-alyx-vr-sound/)
- Ben Lang: "almost every object has a completely unique sound when being shaken," spatialised to the nearer ear. Each force-pull stage has its own sound. — [Road to VR](https://www.roadtovr.com/these-details-make-half-life-alyx-unlike-any-other-vr-game-inside-xr-design/)
- Lone Echo: "the ever-present whir of tiny servos as you flex your robo-appendages" is cited as part of its best-in-class hand presence. — [Shacknews](https://shacknews.com/article/97189/lone-echo-impressions-from-oculus-connect-3)
- Arizona Sunshine 2: sound design is praised consistently across outlets alongside the headshot feel (excerpt-level). — [Road to VR](https://www.roadtovr.com/arizona-sunshine-2-review-quest-pc-vr-psvr-2/)
- Meta: synchronise haptics with audio so they read as "one event," and match haptic intensity to the sound. — [Meta guidelines](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/documentation/unity/unity-haptics-design-guidelines/)

### Inferences
- For a game-agnostic runtime injecting interactions into flat games (which have no grab, holster or mag-insert sounds of their own), a small consistent library of runtime-owned confirmation sounds is worth shipping: grab, release, holster in and out, mag seat, mag eject, chamber or rack, and a failed-action "clunk." Alyx's "few grab sounds" principle suggests consistency beats variety for confirmation cues.
- Trigger each such sound on the same frame as its haptic transient, spatialised at the hand. Scale volume with impact velocity where physics exists.

### Gaps
- None of the retrieved sources detail reload-step audio (mag insert, slide release) in Alyx or other titles, despite those being the obvious confirmation points. I found no published study quantifying audio's effect on grab or reload success rates.

## What flat-to-VR mods get wrong, and what the best ones did right

### Takeaway
Generic injectors give you stereo and head tracking, but the player controller is the weak point. Luke Ross's R.E.A.L. mods deliberately skip motion controls and aim with the head. UEVR's default is input translation rather than hands, so motion-controlled weapons, visible hands, haptics, holsters and manual reloads depend entirely on per-game community profiles, and misalignment is common. The best conversions (Titanfall 2 VR, HL2VR, Skyrim VR with HIGGS/PLANCK/VRIK, FO4VR with FRIK, and the best UEVR profiles) are praised for rebuilding the hand layer: independent articulated hands, per-weapon grip offsets, physical reloads and melee, HUD moved onto hands or weapons, VR-native prompts, and comfort-gated cutscenes.

### Cited Findings
- Luke Ross: the KCD2 R.E.A.L. mod "is not designed for motion controls," and you play it with gamepad or mouse and keyboard. — [Mixed](https://mixed-news.com/en/kingdom-come-deliverance-ii-vr-mod/). Indiana Jones works the same way, gamepad only. — [Mixed](https://mixed-news.com/en/indiana-jones-and-the-great-circle-vr-mod/). Doom: The Dark Ages supports head-tracked aiming with no motion controls. — [Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/doom-the-dark-ages-vr-mod-released-by-luke-ross/amp/). Ross markets gaze aiming as fast and intuitive (excerpt). — [Road to VR](https://www.roadtovr.com/?p=126611)
- UEVR: it "does not automatically convert a flat-screen game into a fully interactive VR experience with hand physics," and grabbing, physical reloads and gestures are "not guaranteed." It works via input translation layers. — [uevr.org](https://uevr.org/?p=193). Advanced 2026 profiles add visible hands, one-handed 6DOF weapons, recoil, haptics, holsters, gestures and manual reload (e.g., Ready or Not, Borderlands 3). — [PCVR Central](https://pcvrcentral.com/guides/best-uevr-games-2026)
- UEVR profile examples: Atomic Heart defaults to headset aiming. Hold right grip to aim from the hand, and the trigger is blocked unless aiming. — [Nexus](https://www.nexusmods.com/atomicheart/mods/234). System Shock adds ADS, aligns projectiles to the sights and fixes motion-control alignment. — [GitHub](https://github.com/Ashok0/SystemShock-UEVR). The MGS Delta plugin adds animated hands with custom per-weapon and per-item poses, plus two-handing by a quick press of the left grip. — [Nexus](https://www.nexusmods.com/metalgearsoliddeltasnakeeater/mods/167?tab=description)
- UEVR community: "worked remarkably well at separating out UI," but the user was waiting for "more community configs." — [Tildes](https://tildes.net/~games/1di9/uevr_is_here_hype_or_legit_its_legit). UploadVR complains about manual mod setup needing UEVR profiles, in contrast to Titanfall 2 VR's installer. — [UploadVR](https://www.uploadvr.com/titanfall-2s-vr-mod-is-stellar-and-its-installer-is-the-best-thing-about-it/)
- Titanfall 2 VR did these right: articulated hands mapped 1:1, per-gun manual reloads, physical punching, holographic sights that work at depth, a Titan cockpit with presence, VR controller glyphs instead of gamepad buttons, and a cutscene toggle (2D panel versus full VR). — [Aftermath](https://aftermath.site/titanfall-2-vr-mod-circuitlord/); [UploadVR](https://www.uploadvr.com/titanfall-2s-vr-mod-is-stellar-and-its-installer-is-the-best-thing-about-it/). What it got wrong: some abilities remain head-aimed, weapon switching is awkward, there is no crosshair or laser option, and forced VR cutscene cameras are intense. — [Aftermath](https://aftermath.site/titanfall-2-vr-mod-circuitlord/); [ResetEra](https://www.resetera.com/threads/titanfall-2-vr-mod-is-now-available.1644223/)
- HL2VR did these right: rebuilt weapon models to be viewable from all angles, added reload gestures, put HUD on the weapons, and offers left-handed mode, a laser sight, a quick-reload option, a vignette and a setup wizard exposing comfort settings. — [Road to VR](https://roadtovr.com/half-life-2-vr-mod-coming-to-oculus-rift-and-htc-vive/); [Road to VR 1.4](https://roadtovr.com/news-bits-hl2vr-1-4-mod-released-best-half-life-2-experience-just-got-better/); [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/658920/HalfLife_2_VR_Mod/); [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/98222-half-life-2-episode-2-vr-mod-arrives.html)
- Bethesda VR mods: HIGGS fixes "poor object interaction & looting." — [Road to VR](https://roadtovr.com/skyrim-vr-mod-higgsvr-hand-gravity-gloves/). VRIK adds a body, holsters and gestures that replace menus. — [Nexus VRIK](https://nexusmods.com/skyrimspecialedition/mods/23416). Idle Hands moves the HUD to the back of the hand and hand-positions every weapon. — [Nexus Idle Hands](https://www.nexusmods.com/fallout4/mods/42922). Vanilla FO4VR's Pip-Boy "can be a pain." — [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/fallout-4-is-impressively-playable-in-vr)

### Inferences
- Evidence-backed priorities for a game-agnostic VR runtime's player controller:
  1. Independent, tracked, articulated hands, with the weapon parented to a per-controller-model grip offset that the user can calibrate.
  2. Aim from the weapon or hand by default, with no head-aim fallback for guns.
  3. A free off-hand always available, plus two-handing with stabilisation (1€-style filter or hold-breath).
  4. Physical but forgiving reload steps with generous snap volumes, plus a quick or arcade fallback.
  5. Body-slot holsters and ammo that never steal an item mid-action.
  6. HUD and ammo info moved to hand or weapon, and prompts remapped to VR glyphs.
  7. Cutscenes on a 2D panel by default, with full-VR camera opt-in.
  8. Comfort options (snap turn, vignette) plus a grab, holster and reload event bus that drives haptics and audio together.
- Of these, items 1, 2, 7 and the prompt remap are what separate "mod" from "native" in reviewers' language (UploadVR and ResetEra both use "feels native").

### Gaps
- I found no retrieved Reddit or Steam threads quantifying UEVR or Luke Ross motion-control complaints (e.g., "weapon glued to view," "HUD stuck to face"). Searches surfaced profile docs, not complaint threads. The specific complaint that HUDs are locked to the face in injectors is not sourced here.
- I couldn't verify any Eurogamer or other critic quotes on Ross's head aiming.
